## 1. Purpose, scope and references

### 1.1 Purpose

This manual defines the integrated quality and environmental management system (IMS) used to operate the Reclaim Resources Wodonga Resource Recovery Facility safely, consistently and in an environmentally sound manner. It establishes the controls needed to:

- accept approved plastic waste from contracted sources;
- recover fit-for-purpose products through thermal depolymerisation;
- meet customer, statutory, regulatory and licence requirements;
- protect the environment, including prevention of pollution;
- manage risks and opportunities;
- evaluate performance and continually improve the IMS.

All personnel and contractors whose work can affect product quality, legal compliance or environmental performance must follow the controls relevant to their work.

### 1.2 IMS scope

The IMS applies to activities controlled or influenced by Reclaim Resources at the Wodonga facility, including:

- supplier and customer engagement;
- waste acceptance, transport interface, receipt, inspection and storage;
- batch preparation, thermal processing, filtration, centrifuging and reprocessing;
- storage, release, sale and dispatch of recovered hydrocarbon products and char;
- management of process gas, water, residues, emissions and other wastes;
- laboratory sampling, testing and external laboratory services;
- plant operation, inspection, maintenance and contractor activities;
- emergency preparedness, incident response, complaints and corrective action;
- supporting purchasing, training, document control and recordkeeping activities.

The facility operates under EPA Victoria Pilot Project Licence **PPL000300013**, currently recorded as expiring 31 December 2027. The CEO manages the licence (§4.4). The current licence held in the compliance register is authoritative; personnel must not rely on this summary for operating limits.

**Organisational boundary.** The facility is operated by Reclaim Resources Pty Ltd (see the [Organisational Structure](../assets/docs/Organisational%20Structure.md)). Group functions that Reclaim Resources provides centrally to the facility are within the scope of this IMS to the extent that they affect the facility. These functions are licensing, laboratory testing and product release, R&D, recruitment and training, feedstock and offtake agreements, and finance. Other Reclaim Resources sites, including the South Australian plant, are **outside** this IMS. Those sites are subject to the law of their own jurisdiction, such as the *Work Health and Safety Act 2012* (SA) and EPA South Australia requirements, and need their own documented arrangements.

Hydroxy Systems Australia supplies the thermolysis plant under licence from Hydroxy Systems India and provides plant service. It is an external provider controlled under §7.4.

No ISO 9001 requirement is declared not applicable in this manual. If management determines that design and development is not performed, it must document and approve the justification without affecting the facility's ability to provide conforming products or satisfy customers.

## 3. Context of the organisation

### 3.1 Facility and process

The facility supports the circular economy by recovering value from selected plastic waste that might otherwise be disposed of. A steel-framed building houses a pre-packaged plant that thermally depolymerises approved plastic feedstocks into hydrocarbon fractions, carbonised char and non-condensable gas.

The process is batch-based and generally runs for 14–16 hours at controlled temperatures from approximately 200 °C to 500 °C. Approved operating recipes and control algorithms are selected for the feedstock. Volatile fractions are separated and condensed into hydrocarbon fractions. Product is collected in intermediate tanks and centrifuged to remove water before transfer to identified steel storage tanks. Non-condensable gas, historically estimated at approximately 2% of input mass, is used as process fuel where permitted by the approved plant configuration. Process water is treated and may only be discharged or reused when the applicable approval criteria are met.

The high-level operational sequence is:

1. supplier and waste-stream approval;
2. feedstock receipt, identification, inspection and acceptance;
3. segregated storage and batch preparation;
4. controlled batch processing;
5. filtration and centrifuging;
6. product testing, storage, release and dispatch;
7. reprocessing or control of nonconforming material;
8. management of water, char, residues and other outputs.

Current process flow diagrams, facility plans, tank identification and drainage plans must be controlled in the document register and available where needed.

### 3.2 Internal and external issues

At least annually, and when a material change occurs, the management review evaluates issues that can affect the intended outcomes of the IMS.

| Context | Issues to consider |

The organisation has determined climate change to be relevant because heat, storm, flood, fire, power interruption and water availability may affect storage, emergency controls, production continuity and environmental performance. ### 3.4 IMS processes and interactions

The IMS uses the Plan–Do–Check–Act cycle:

```text
PLAN   Context and obligations → risks/aspects → objectives and controls
  ↓
DO     Supplier approval → receipt → processing → testing/release → dispatch
  ↓
CHECK  Monitoring → compliance evaluation → audit → management review
  ↓
ACT    Correction → corrective action → controlled change → improvement
  └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────↺
```

Each process owner must define the process inputs, outputs, responsibilities, acceptance criteria, resources, controls, measures and retained records in the applicable procedure or process map.

## 4. Leadership and governance

### 4.1 Leadership commitment

Top management is accountable for the effectiveness of the IMS. It will:

- establish policy and measurable objectives compatible with strategic direction and context;
- integrate quality and environmental requirements into business and operational decisions;
- provide competent people, infrastructure, monitoring equipment and financial resources;
- promote process-based thinking, life-cycle thinking and risk-based decision-making;
- ensure customer and compliance requirements are understood and fulfilled;
- support pollution prevention, protection of the environment and continual improvement;
- communicate the importance of effective management and conformity;
- assign authority, remove barriers and support personnel who contribute to IMS effectiveness;
- review performance and ensure the IMS achieves its intended outcomes.

### 4.2 Integrated quality and environmental policy

Reclaim Resources is committed to recovering useful resources from approved plastic waste in a controlled manner that consistently meets customer and applicable requirements. We will:

- understand customer needs and provide traceable, fit-for-purpose products;
- comply with applicable legal, licence, contractual and other obligations;
- protect the environment, prevent pollution and minimise adverse impacts from emissions, discharges, waste, resource use, transport, noise, odour and emergencies;
- apply a life-cycle perspective when selecting feedstocks, suppliers, equipment, packaging, transport and product uses;
- use energy, water and materials efficiently and pursue practical waste reduction and recovery;
- consult, train and support workers and contractors whose activities affect quality or environmental performance;
- set and review measurable quality and environmental objectives; and
- continually improve the suitability, adequacy and effectiveness of the IMS and facility performance.

The Operations Director approves the policy on behalf of top management. It is communicated during induction and toolbox meetings, displayed at the facility, made available to relevant interested parties, and reviewed during management review.

### 4.3 Customer focus

The Operations Director, with the Sales Director, ensures that customer and applicable statutory requirements are identified, reviewed and met. Risks that could affect conformity or customer satisfaction are controlled. Performance is evaluated through test results, delivery performance, complaints, returns, repeat business and customer feedback.

## 5. Planning

### 5.1 Risks and opportunities

The Facility Manager maintains an integrated Risk and Opportunity Register addressing:

- context and interested-party requirements;
- customer satisfaction and product conformity;
- environmental aspects and compliance obligations;
- process, supplier, laboratory and infrastructure performance;
- emergencies, climate-related disruption and business continuity;
- opportunities to improve yield, consistency, resource efficiency and pollution prevention.

Risks are scored using the approved consequence and likelihood matrix. Existing controls, residual rating, further action, owner and due date are recorded. High or unacceptable risks require treatment and management approval before the affected activity proceeds. Effectiveness is reviewed after treatment, after incidents or changes, and at least annually.

### 5.2 Environmental aspects

The Facility Manager maintains an Environmental Aspects and Impacts Register covering normal, abnormal and reasonably foreseeable emergency conditions and a life-cycle perspective. Significance is determined using documented criteria that consider scale, severity, duration, frequency or likelihood, legal exposure, stakeholder concern and the organisation's degree of control or influence.

Initial aspects requiring assessment include:

| Activity / condition | Potential impact | Principal controls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Waste receipt and storage | Spill, litter, odour, fire, contaminated or prohibited material | Approved suppliers/streams; acceptance criteria; inspection; containment; inventory and storage limits; quarantine. |
| Thermal depolymerisation | Air emissions, odour, fire/explosion, energy use, off-specification output | Approved recipe and limits; interlocks and alarms; trained operators; preventive maintenance; emission controls; batch records; emergency shutdown. |
| Product and chemical storage/loading | Soil, stormwater or groundwater contamination; vapour and fire | Compatible tanks; identification; bunding; inspections; transfer supervision; overfill protection; spill response. |
| Process water treatment/discharge or irrigation | Water or soil pollution; breach of discharge criteria | Segregation; treatment; sampling; approved discharge/reuse criteria; volume records; hold-and-release controls. |
| Char, filter residue and other wastes | Resource loss, contamination, dust or unlawful disposal | Classification; covered and labelled storage; approved transporter/receiver; tracking records; recovery preference. |
| Maintenance and cleaning | Oil/chemical spill, contaminated wash water, emissions | Permit/isolation controls; trays and bunding; approved chemicals; waste segregation; contractor induction. |
| Transport and dispatch | Emissions, spill, traffic and downstream misuse | Approved carriers; secure loads; dispatch checks; emergency information; customer/use review. |
| Abnormal weather, utility loss or control failure | Overflow, uncontrolled shutdown, fire or loss of containment | Weather preparedness; capacity checks; backup/controlled shutdown; alarms; emergency response and drills. |

Significant aspects are linked to operational controls, competence, monitoring, emergency arrangements and objectives.

### 5.3 Compliance obligations

The Legal and Other Requirements Register identifies each applicable obligation, its source, activities affected, required action, responsible owner, evidence, evaluation frequency and status. It includes, as applicable:

- EPA licence and permission conditions;
- environment protection, waste, dangerous goods and contaminated land requirements;
- trade waste, water, planning, building and fire requirements;
- mandatory monitoring, record retention and incident/reporting timeframes;
- customer contracts, product specifications and voluntary commitments.

The Facility Manager reviews the register quarterly and whenever notified of change, and tells the CEO about any change that affects the EPA licence. Legal advice or regulator clarification is obtained where applicability is uncertain.

### 5.4 Objectives and plans

Top management approves annual objectives after reviewing baseline data, significant aspects, compliance obligations, risks and business priorities. Each objective must have a measure, baseline, target, action, owner, resources, due date and review frequency. Results are evaluated at quarterly performance meetings and formal management review.

The initial objective framework is:

| Area | Measure | Target-setting basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product quality | Batches meeting release specification first time; customer complaints | Baseline first three months, then approved improvement target. |
| Waste acceptance | Rejected/non-approved loads and traceability completeness | 100% acceptance decisions and traceability recorded; trend contamination. |
| Compliance | Overdue compliance actions and substantiated breaches | Zero overdue statutory actions and zero preventable breaches. |
| Pollution prevention | Loss-of-containment events and uncontrolled discharges | Zero uncontrolled discharges; investigate and reduce spills. |
| Resource efficiency | Product yield, process energy and water per accepted tonne | Establish reliable baseline, then approve technically feasible reductions. |
| Reliability | Preventive maintenance completed on time; unplanned downtime | Target based on criticality and baseline performance. |
| Competence | Required training current | 100% current before unsupervised work. |

Numerical targets not imposed by law or licence must be approved after credible baseline data is available; they must not be retrofitted to results.

### 5.5 Planning and control of change

Changes to feedstock, supplier, customer use, product specification, recipe, equipment, software, tank service, monitoring method, chemical, layout, staffing, legal requirement or outsourced process must be reviewed before implementation. The change review considers:

- purpose, consequences and risks;
- environmental aspects and compliance obligations;
- customer and product requirements;
- plant capacity, compatibility and integrity;
- competence, documents, monitoring and emergency response;
- resources, responsibilities and verification/validation required.

The Facility Manager authorises routine changes within delegation. Changes affecting permissions, significant aspects, product safety, critical operating limits or major capital require Operations Director approval and, where required, regulator or customer approval. Changes to the EPA licence or other permissions are applied for by the CEO. Records include the decision, conditions and post-implementation review.

## 6. Support

### 6.1 Resources and infrastructure

Management provides and maintains suitable buildings, bunds, drainage, plant, tanks, utilities, process controls, laboratory equipment, communications, fire protection, spill equipment and information systems. The Asset and Maintenance Register identifies critical assets, inspection and preventive maintenance requirements, responsible persons and records.

The work environment is controlled for cleanliness, lighting, temperature, access, housekeeping and other conditions necessary for reliable operations and valid testing.

### 6.2 Monitoring and measuring resources

Equipment used to determine acceptance, process control, licence compliance or product conformity is uniquely identified and included in the Calibration and Verification Register. Required range, accuracy, method, frequency, status and traceability are defined.

The Quality Analyst maintains the register. Equipment that is overdue, damaged or outside tolerance is removed from use or clearly controlled. The Chief Chemist assesses the validity of previous results, identifies affected material or reports, and initiates correction and corrective action where necessary. Calibration certificates and impact assessments are retained.

### 6.3 Organisational knowledge

Required knowledge includes licence conditions, approved waste streams, feedstock compatibility, recipes, critical limits, failure modes, sampling and release criteria, environmental controls and emergency response. It is maintained through controlled manuals, work instructions, training, handover records, maintenance history, incident learning and competent supervision. Knowledge needs are reviewed before change and when experienced personnel leave or roles change.

### 6.4 Competence, awareness and communication

The Training and Competency Matrix defines required education, training, licences, experience and authorisations for each role and for each Operator function. It also records which functions each Operator is authorised to perform, and any written delegations from the Facility Manager (§4.4). High Risk Work Licences are sighted and recorded before an Operator performs licensed work (OHS Regulations 2017, Part 3.6). Personnel may work unsupervised only after competence has been evaluated through observation, questioning, record review or practical assessment. Refresher training is provided at defined intervals and after material change, incident or demonstrated deficiency.

Induction and ongoing communication cover:

- the integrated policy and relevant objectives;
- contribution to product conformity and environmental performance;
- significant aspects, operational controls and compliance obligations relevant to the role;
- consequences of deviating from approved controls;
- incident, complaint, defect and improvement reporting;
- emergency alarms, shutdown, evacuation and spill response.

The Facility Manager defines what will be communicated, when, to whom, how and by whom in the Communication Register. External statements and statutory notifications are made only by authorised personnel, are consistent and reliable, and are retained where required.

### 6.5 Documented information

Controlled documents must have an identifier, title, owner, version, approval, effective date and review status. Before issue they are reviewed for suitability and adequacy. The document owner controls access, distribution, retrieval, storage, change, retention and disposition.

Only current approved instructions may be used. Superseded documents are removed from points of use or marked to prevent unintended use. External documents, including licences, standards, safety data sheets, specifications and manufacturer manuals, are identified and version-controlled where needed.

Records must be legible, attributable, contemporaneous, original or a verified copy, accurate, retrievable and protected from unauthorised alteration or loss. Retention periods are set in the Records Register according to legal, licence, customer and business requirements.

## 7. Operation

### 7.1 Operational planning and control

The Facility Manager ensures work is performed under controlled conditions using current procedures, competent personnel, suitable equipment, acceptance criteria and records. Daily pre-start and end-of-shift checks verify containment, alarms, tank capacity, housekeeping, emergency equipment and abnormalities. Shift handovers identify operating status, quarantined materials, alarms, permits, maintenance and pending actions.

Activities associated with significant environmental aspects are controlled through documented criteria. Outsourced processes remain controlled by the facility, with requirements communicated to relevant suppliers and contractors.

### 7.2 Customer and product requirements

Before accepting an order or committing product, authorised personnel review:

- stated and intended-use requirements;
- product specification, quantity, delivery and documentation;
- applicable statutory and regulatory requirements;
- the facility's capability to meet the requirements;
- differences from previous quotations or contracts.

Requirements and changes are confirmed and communicated to affected personnel. Customer property, specifications and confidential information are identified, protected and reported if lost, damaged or unsuitable. Customer communications cover enquiries, orders, changes, product information, feedback, complaints and contingency actions where relevant.

### 7.3 Design and development

New or materially changed products, formulations, intended uses or process recipes are managed under a documented design and development plan proportionate to risk. The Chief Chemist leads design and development (R&D) under the oversight of the Technical Director. Changes are introduced at the facility only through §5.5. The plan defines stages, responsibilities, review, verification, validation and approval.

Inputs include customer and functional requirements, legal requirements, feedstock characteristics, prior designs, potential failure consequences and environmental life-cycle considerations. Outputs define acceptance criteria, monitoring, operating information and product characteristics needed for safe and intended use. Reviews involve competent functions independent of the work where practical. Changes are identified, evaluated and approved, and adverse impacts are prevented.

Routine production to an existing approved recipe and specification is operational control, not a new design activity.

### 7.4 Control of external providers

Suppliers and contractors are selected, approved, monitored and periodically re-evaluated according to the risk of their product or service. Controls apply to feedstock suppliers, carriers, laboratories, waste receivers, calibration providers, maintenance contractors and critical parts or chemicals.

Purchase or service information specifies applicable acceptance, competence, licence, environmental, traceability and reporting requirements. Incoming products and services are verified before use. Poor performance is recorded and can result in increased inspection, corrective action, suspension or removal from the Approved Provider Register.

### 7.5 Feedstock approval, receipt and storage

Only waste types and quantities authorised by the current EPA permission and approved by the Facility Manager may be accepted.

Before first receipt, and after any source or composition change, the facility documents:

- waste generator and source activity;
- classification and evidence that the waste is authorised;
- expected composition, contaminants and variability;
- compatibility with the process, storage and other materials;
- sampling or analysis required;
- intended recovered product and legitimate market/use;
- contractual controls, transport and traceability arrangements.

At receipt, the operator:

1. confirms supplier, transport and booking details;
2. verifies waste description, source, quantity and required documentation;
3. inspects the load and, where required, samples/tests it using the approved plan;
4. checks inventory, storage and licensed capacity;
5. records acceptance, conditional acceptance, quarantine or rejection;
6. assigns traceable identification and directs material to the designated area.

Suspected prohibited, incompatible, unidentified or contaminated material is isolated where safe, not processed, and escalated to the Facility Manager. Rejection and any regulator notification are documented. Stored feedstock is identified, segregated, contained, protected from weather and ignition, and managed using defined inventory rotation and maximum holding limits.

### 7.6 Batch preparation and processing

The operator prepares an authorised batch sheet linking feedstock lots, quantities, recipe, equipment, tanks and intended product specification. Before start, the operator confirms:

- accepted and compatible feedstock;
- available receiving and product tank capacity;
- clean, serviceable and correctly configured plant;
- current calibration and completed critical maintenance;
- functioning interlocks, alarms, ventilation and emission controls;
- closed drains/containment and available emergency equipment;
- current work instruction and authorised recipe.

Critical parameters, alarms, adjustments, samples, yields, downtime and deviations are recorded during the batch. Operators must not bypass an interlock or exceed an approved limit without written engineering assessment and authorisation permitted by law and manufacturer requirements. On abnormal conditions, the operator moves the plant to a safe state, contains environmental impacts, identifies affected material and reports the event.

### 7.7 Identification, traceability and preservation

Unique lot or batch identification links incoming source and quantity to processing records, test results, storage location, release decision and dispatch/customer. Status is visible as accepted, awaiting test, quarantined, released, rework or rejected.

Feedstock, intermediate and final product are preserved through compatible containers and tanks, clear labels, closed transfers, contamination controls, inventory checks and suitable storage conditions. Tank or line changeover is verified to prevent mixing. Traceability is tested periodically through a mock recall or mass-balance exercise.

### 7.8 Monitoring, sampling and product release

Sampling and testing follow controlled methods that define location, frequency, equipment, preservation, chain of custody, test method, acceptance criteria and responsibility. External compliance or specification testing is assigned to an appropriately accredited laboratory where required.

Product remains identified and quarantined until all specified checks are complete. Only the Chief Chemist, or a Quality Analyst holding written delegated authority, may release it, with records showing conformity evidence, decision, date and identity. Urgent release before planned verification is prohibited unless specifically authorised, legally permissible, traceable and recoverable; product subject to mandatory criteria is never released without required evidence.

Process water may be discharged, sent to trade waste or used for irrigation only under a current permission and after documented confirmation that applicable quality, location and volume criteria are satisfied.

### 7.9 Nonconforming outputs

Nonconforming feedstock, intermediate product, final product, water or residue is promptly identified and controlled to prevent unintended processing, release, discharge or use. The authorised disposition is one of:

- correction, treatment or reprocessing followed by full re-verification;
- segregation and use under an approved concession where lawful and agreed by the customer;
- return to supplier;
- reclassification and transfer to an authorised receiver;
- disposal through an approved provider.

Records identify the nonconformity, quantity, containment, evaluation, disposition, approvals and verification. If nonconformity is found after dispatch, the Facility Manager assesses customer, legal, environmental and recall/withdrawal actions immediately.

### 7.10 Environmental operational controls

The following minimum controls apply, supplemented by aspect-specific procedures:

- inspect tanks, bunds, valves, hoses, drainage and storage areas at defined frequencies;
- maintain adequate containment and safe working capacity before transfer or rainfall;
- supervise transfers, verify destination and capacity, use secure connections and record quantities;
- keep stormwater, process water and contaminated water segregated;
- control odour, noise, dust, vapour and emissions within approved conditions;
- label chemicals and wastes, maintain current safety data sheets and segregate incompatibles;
- apply the waste hierarchy: avoid, reduce, reuse, recycle, recover, treat, then dispose;
- use authorised transporters and receiving facilities and retain movement evidence;
- consider energy, water, packaging, transport and downstream information in purchasing and customer decisions;
- stop and report any activity that threatens an uncontrolled discharge or licence breach.

### 7.11 Maintenance and return to service

Maintenance is planned according to asset criticality, manufacturer instructions, statutory inspections, condition and failure history. Defects affecting quality, containment, monitoring or legal compliance are prioritised and the equipment is isolated where continued use is unacceptable.

Maintenance work controls energy isolation, permits, parts, contamination, chemicals, spills and resulting waste. Before return to service, a person authorised by the Facility Manager verifies guards and controls, leak integrity, calibration where affected, removal of tools and isolations, housekeeping, functional performance and documentation. Temporary repairs are identified, risk-assessed and assigned a permanent correction due date.

### 7.12 Emergency preparedness and response

The Emergency Plan addresses credible events including fire or explosion, spill or loss of containment, uncontrolled emission or odour, prohibited waste, process runaway, utility/control failure, tank overfill, severe weather, flood, medical emergency and transport incident.

The Facility Manager is the Chief Warden. When the Facility Manager is not on site, a trained Operator appointed as warden takes command until the Facility Manager or emergency services take over (§4.4 Delegation). For each scenario the plan defines alarms, command, shutdown/isolation, evacuation, containment, emergency services, regulator and interested-party notifications, recovery and post-event evaluation. Current plans show exits, assembly areas, drainage, isolation points, hazardous materials, fire controls and spill equipment.

Emergency equipment is inspected at planned intervals. Personnel and relevant contractors receive training. Exercises are conducted at least twice each year, including at least one spill/loss-of-containment scenario, and after significant change where warranted. Actual responses and exercises are evaluated and plans revised when deficiencies are found. Notifications follow the current legal and licence timeframes in the compliance register.

### 7.13 Complaints and incidents

Any worker receiving a complaint records the date, source, contact details where offered, issue, location and immediate action, then promptly refers it to the Facility Manager. Complaints are acknowledged, investigated objectively, linked to operating and monitoring data, and closed with the complainant where appropriate. Privacy is protected.

Incidents, near misses, licence deviations and complaints with possible environmental or product impact are contained and assessed immediately. Evidence is preserved, notification obligations are checked, causes are investigated proportionately, and corrective actions are tracked. No person may delay a mandatory notification pending completion of an investigation. The Facility Manager makes statutory notifications: to WorkSafe for notifiable incidents (OHS Act s.38) and to EPA as soon as practicable for notifiable pollution incidents (EP Act s.32). The Facility Manager informs the Operations Director, and informs the CEO of any matter affecting the licence. If the Facility Manager cannot be reached, the most senior person on site notifies.

### 7.14 Site security and housekeeping

Access is restricted to authorised and inducted persons. Visitors sign in and are escorted as required. Keys, access codes and critical system permissions are issued by role and withdrawn when no longer needed. Security incidents are recorded.

Work areas, access routes, labels, spill controls and emergency equipment are kept clean, unobstructed and serviceable. Operators complete housekeeping checks each shift. Accumulated waste, leaks, damaged containers and unidentified materials are corrected or escalated promptly.

## 8. Performance evaluation

### 8.1 Monitoring, measurement, analysis and evaluation

The IMS Monitoring Plan defines what is measured, method, criteria, location, frequency, responsibility, analysis and retained record. It includes, as applicable:

- licence and trade-waste parameters, emissions and discharge volumes;
- accepted, rejected, processed, recovered, reworked and disposed quantities;
- mass balance and yield by batch;
- product conformity and laboratory performance;
- energy and water consumption per accepted tonne;
- spills, abnormal events, complaints and corrective actions;
- supplier, delivery, maintenance, training and objective performance.

Data is checked for validity and trended. Adverse trends trigger investigation before a limit or specification is exceeded. The Facility Manager prepares a monthly performance report for the operational review.

### 8.2 Customer satisfaction

Customer perception is monitored through complaints, returns, specification performance, delivery results, feedback and commercial review. Trends and significant issues are evaluated and acted upon.

### 8.3 Evaluation of compliance

The Facility Manager evaluates compliance at planned intervals and at least annually, with higher-risk obligations checked more frequently. Evaluation uses records, inspections, interviews, monitoring and direct comparison with each obligation. Results, evidence, status and required actions are retained. Suspected noncompliance is controlled under section 9 and reported where required.

### 8.4 Internal audit

An annual risk-based audit program covers all ISO 9001, ISO 14001, legal, licence and IMS requirements over the audit cycle. Frequency considers process importance, significant aspects, change, prior results and performance.

Auditors are competent and independent of the work audited where practical. Each audit defines criteria and scope, uses objective evidence, reports conclusions to relevant management and tracks corrections and corrective actions to verified closure. Audit records include the program, plan, evidence, report and follow-up.

### 8.5 Management review

Operational performance is reviewed monthly. The Operations Director chairs a formal IMS management review on behalf of top management at least annually. The Facility Manager and the Chief Chemist attend, and others attend as needed. Inputs include:

- status of previous actions;
- changes in context, interested-party requirements, climate relevance and compliance obligations;
- customer satisfaction and relevant communications/complaints;
- achievement of quality and environmental objectives;
- process performance, product conformity and environmental trends;
- monitoring results and evaluation of compliance;
- incidents, nonconformities and corrective actions;
- audit results and provider performance;
- adequacy of people, infrastructure, knowledge and financial resources;
- effectiveness of actions for risks, opportunities and significant aspects;
- opportunities for continual improvement.

Outputs include decisions and actions concerning improvement, IMS changes, objectives, resources and any implications for strategic direction. Minutes identify owners and due dates and are retained as controlled records.

## 9. Improvement

### 9.1 Nonconformity and corrective action

When a nonconformity occurs, the responsible manager will:

1. control and correct it and address consequences, including environmental mitigation and affected product;
2. determine notification and communication requirements;
3. evaluate the need for action to eliminate causes by reviewing the event, determining causes and checking for similar conditions;
4. implement proportionate action without undue delay;
5. review effectiveness using evidence that recurrence risk has reduced;
6. update risks, aspects, controls and documents where necessary;
7. retain records of the event, action and effectiveness decision.

Correction alone does not close a recurring or significant issue. Root-cause depth is proportionate to actual and potential impact.

### 9.2 Continual improvement

Improvement opportunities are identified from data trends, employee suggestions, customer feedback, audits, compliance evaluations, incidents, maintenance, technology and management review. They are prioritised according to benefit, risk, compliance, environmental impact and resources. Completed changes are evaluated for intended and unintended results.

## 12. Implementation actions

Before this manual is approved as implemented, top management must ensure that:

- the legal and other requirements register has been reviewed by a competent person against the current licence and facility activities;
- approved site plans, process flow, tank schedule, operating limits, acceptance criteria and product specifications are controlled and available;
- risk and opportunity and environmental aspect assessments are complete and significant aspects identified;
- measurable objectives, baselines, numerical targets, owners and resources are approved;
- procedures and forms referenced by this manual exist at points of use and reflect actual practice;
- competent role-holders, deputies, emergency contacts and notification responsibilities are assigned;
- monitoring and calibration requirements and record-retention periods are defined;
- emergency scenarios have been exercised and resulting actions closed;
- personnel have been trained and competence verified;
- an internal audit has tested implementation and objective evidence; and
- top management has completed and recorded the initial management review.
