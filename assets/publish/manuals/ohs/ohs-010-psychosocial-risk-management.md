## 1. Purpose
To identify psychosocial hazards and to eliminate, or reduce so far as is reasonably practicable, the risks they create to workers' psychological and physical health. This procedure meets the *Occupational Health and Safety (Psychological Health) Regulations 2025* (r.14–16) and follows the WorkSafe *Psychological health compliance code* (Edition 1, 2025).

## 2. Scope
Covers all employees, labour-hire workers and contractors (for matters Reclaim Resources controls), in all work areas and shifts.

## 3. Definitions
**Psychosocial hazard:** any factor or factors in work design, systems of work, management of work, carrying out of the work, or personal or work-related interactions that may arise in the working environment and may cause an employee to have one or more negative psychological responses that create a risk to their health or safety.

## 6. Risk Management Process
Use [F-009 Psychosocial Hazards Prevention Plan](../../forms/f09-psychosocial-prevention-plan.md) to record each step. It is based on the WorkSafe prevention plan template. Prepare one **site-wide plan** for hazards common across the site (e.g. bullying, sexual harassment). Prepare **area or role-specific plans** where exposures differ (e.g. control room night operators for isolated work and low job demands; operators for traumatic events).

### 6.1 Step 1: Identify Hazards (r.14)
Use several sources. **Do not record information that could identify individuals.**
- Consultation with workers and HSRs (toolbox talks, H&S committee, focus groups).
- A validated, anonymous survey (e.g. People at Work, or a COPSOQ-based tool) every **[TBC – 2] years**.
- Hazard, incident and complaint reports. Workers' compensation claims, including rejected claims.
- Leave, overtime, turnover and exit-interview data. Roster and fatigue data ([OHS-012](ohs-012-fatigue-management.md)).
- Workplace observation and "walk and talk" inspections ([OHS-007](ohs-007-workplace-inspection.md)).
- EAP de-identified utilisation reports ([OHS-008](ohs-008-mental-health-and-support-program.md)).
- External information: WorkSafe guidance, and industry incidents.

### 6.2 Step 2: Assess the Risk
For each hazard, consider:
- how likely exposure is, and whether some workers are more exposed (e.g. night shift, new starters, young workers, women in a male-dominated team);
- how often and how long people are exposed;
- how severe the consequences could be, and whether some workers could be affected more severely;
- whether hazards interact or combine (e.g. high demands + poor support → conflict and bullying; fatigue + isolated work).

Rate the risk with the facility risk matrix (FOM-001 §5.1) and record it in the Risk and Opportunity Register.

### 6.3 Step 3: Control Risks (r.15)
1. **Eliminate** the risk, so far as is reasonably practicable.
2. If it cannot be eliminated, **reduce** the risk so far as is reasonably practicable by **altering** one or more of: the **management of work**, the **plant**, the **systems of work**, the **work design**, or the **workplace environment**.
3. **Information, instruction or training** may be used:
   - **only in combination** with the controls in point 2, and **never as the predominant control** (r.15(4)); or
   - **on its own only** where none of the controls in point 2 are reasonably practicable (r.15(3)). Document the reasons.
4. Make sure controls are properly installed, used and maintained.

**Example controls for this site:**
| Hazard | Controls that alter work (predominant) | Supporting information or training |
|--------|-----------------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| High job demands | Staffing set by batch schedule; no batch turnaround in the last 2 h of a shift; production targets set with operator input; extra cover during upsets | Workload escalation process |
| Remote / isolated work | **No lone work during lid removal, lifting or transfers**; scheduled check-in calls every **[TBC] h** at night; lone-worker duress device; CCTV of plant areas monitored | Lone-worker procedure training |
| Traumatic events | Engineering and process safety controls (WI-001 to WI-010); emergency drills; **critical incident response with immediate psychological first aid and EAP**; follow-up check-ins at 1 week and 1 month | Trauma awareness for supervisors |
| Sexual harassment / gendered violence | Separate, lockable amenities and change rooms; mixed-gender crews where practicable; well-lit car park and paths; clear consequences | Respectful workplace training; bystander training |
| Low role clarity | Position descriptions; RACI for stop-work, restart and hold-point authority (P-001 §5) | WI training |
| Poor change management | Psychosocial impact considered in every change assessment (FOM-001 §5.5), with consultation **before** decisions | Change briefings |
| Low job demands (control room) | Task rotation; meaningful secondary tasks (record review, trend analysis); alarm management | — |
| Poor environmental conditions | Air-conditioned control room and crib room; noise and odour controls; PPE that fits | — |

### 6.4 Step 4: Review and Revise
See Section 7.

## 7. Review and Revision Triggers (r.16)
Review the control measures, and revise them if they are not adequately controlling the risk:
1. **before** any change to a thing, process or system of work that is likely to change psychosocial risks (e.g. new rosters, staffing changes, new plant, restructures);
2. if **new or additional information** about a psychosocial hazard becomes available;
3. if an employee, or someone on their behalf, **reports a psychological injury or a psychosocial hazard**;
4. after any **notifiable incident** (OHS Act Part 5) involving a psychosocial hazard;
5. if the controls **do not adequately control** the risk;
6. if an **HSR requests a review** on reasonable grounds (r.16(2)).

Otherwise, review prevention plans at least **annually**. Record every review in F-009 Step 4.

## 8. Consultation
Consult employees and HSRs at every step: identifying hazards, assessing risks, deciding controls, reviewing controls, and proposing changes (OHS Act s.35). Where HSRs exist, consultation must involve them. Record consultation in F-009.

## 9. Reporting Psychosocial Hazards
Workers can report psychosocial hazards to the Facility Manager, their HSR, a contact officer or the HR Manager, on F-004, or anonymously through **[TBC – anonymous reporting channel]**. Reports are handled confidentially. Bullying, harassment and violence reports follow [OHS-009](ohs-009-bullying-and-harassment.md) and [OHS-011](ohs-011-occupational-violence.md).
