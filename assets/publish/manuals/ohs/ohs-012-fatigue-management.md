## 1. Purpose
To prevent harm from work-related fatigue. The thermolysis cycle runs for 14–16 hours and involves safety-critical tasks: crane lifts, lid removal at temperature, valve line-ups, and emergency response. Fatigue directly increases the risk of serious incidents in these tasks.

## 2. Scope
Covers all employees, labour-hire workers and contractors, including overtime, on-call duties, call-outs and work-related travel.

## 3. Definition
**Work-related fatigue:** an acute or ongoing state of physical, mental or emotional exhaustion that stops people from working safely (WorkSafe Victoria). Being awake for 17 hours impairs performance about as much as a blood alcohol concentration (BAC) of 0.05. Being awake for 20 hours is about the same as 0.1.

## 4. Hours of Work Standards
These limits come from the WorkSafe *Work-related fatigue: A guide for employers* risk indicators. **[TBC: confirm against the enterprise agreement or modern award.]**

| Parameter | Standard | Absolute limit (needs Facility Manager approval and extra controls) |
|-----------|----------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Shift length, including overtime and handover | ≤ 10 h | **12 h** (never exceed) |
| Shift length + commute | ≤ 11 h | 13 h |
| Break between shifts | ≥ 12 h | **10 h minimum** |
| Weekly hours | ≤ 48 h | 56 h in any one week (no more than once every 4 weeks) |
| Consecutive night shifts | ≤ 4 | 5 |
| Consecutive shifts (any type) | ≤ 6, then at least 2 days off | 7 |
| Rest breaks | 30 min meal break for every 5 h worked, plus short breaks (e.g. 10 min every 2 h) | — |
| Roster rotation | **Forward** (day → afternoon → night) | — |

**Batch scheduling:** schedule batch turnaround tasks (WI-001 to WI-005 and WI-009 to WI-010) so that lid removal, lifting and pump-out are **not done in the last 2 hours of a 12-hour shift or between 02:00 and 06:00** where practicable.

## 5. Responsibilities
| Role | Responsibility |
|------|----------------|
| Facility Manager | Designs rosters in consultation with workers. Monitors actual hours against planned hours **monthly**. Approves any exceedance. |
| Facility Manager | Checks fitness for work at the start of shift. Makes sure breaks are taken. Reallocates safety-critical tasks from fatigued workers. Does not pressure anyone to work extra hours. |
| Workers | Use time off to rest and sleep (7–8 hours). Report fatigue, a second job, or long commutes that affect rest. Do not do safety-critical tasks if impaired. |

## 6. Controls (hierarchy)
| Control | Measures |
|---------|----------|
| Eliminate / alter work design | Enough staff to cover leave and the batch schedule without excessive overtime. Automated monitoring and alarms during the run, so there is less need for continuous manual vigilance. Batch timing planned around crew availability. |
| Systems of work | Hours limits (Section 4). Forward-rotating roster. Restrictions on shift swaps, so swaps cannot breach the limits. On-call and call-out rules: if called out, ensure a 10 h break before the next shift. Night shift: **no lone safety-critical tasks**. |
| Environment | Air-conditioned, well-lit control room. Rest area and crib room with healthy food and water. Bright lighting on night shift. |
| Travel | Do not drive home after a 12 h shift if fatigued; a taxi or accommodation is provided. Work travel is counted as work time. |
| Information & training (supporting) | Fatigue awareness at induction. Training the Facility Manager and delegated Operators to recognise signs of fatigue. |

## 7. Fatigue Reporting and Response
1. A worker who feels fatigued, or notices fatigue in a colleague (yawning, micro-sleeps, slow reactions, errors, irritability), tells the Facility Manager (or the delegated Operator if the Facility Manager is off site). **There is no penalty for reporting.**
2. The Facility Manager (or delegated Operator) assesses the worker using the checklist below and takes action:

| Question (pre-shift / during shift) | Yes / No |
|-------------------------------------|----------|
| Less than 5 hours' sleep in the last 24 hours? | |
| Less than 12 hours' sleep in the last 48 hours? | |
| Awake for more than 16 hours by the end of the planned shift? | |
| Showing signs of fatigue? | |

Any **Yes** → do not assign safety-critical tasks (crane, lid removal, valve line-up, transfers). Assign low-risk duties, rest breaks or a nap (≤ 20 min), or send the worker home safely. Record the case on F-004 if it relates to work hours.

3. Trend fatigue reports and hours exceedances. Review the roster and controls ([OHS-010](ohs-010-psychosocial-risk-management.md): high job demands, low job demands, isolated work).

## 8. Monitoring
- Monthly report of actual against planned hours, exceedances, overtime per person, and fatigue reports.
- Fatigue considered in every incident investigation ([OHS-004](ohs-004-incident-reporting-and-investigation.md)).
