## 1. Purpose
To promote good mental health, to make sure workers can get early, confidential support, and to respond well when someone is struggling or has been exposed to a traumatic event.

**This program supports, but does not replace, the duty to prevent harm by controlling psychosocial hazards at their source** ([OHS-010](ohs-010-psychosocial-risk-management.md)). Wellbeing activities and support services are not risk controls for work-related hazards.

## 2. Scope
Covers all employees and their immediate family members (for EAP access). Covers labour-hire workers and contractors for critical incident support.

## 3. Program Elements
### 3.1 Prevent (link to OHS-010)
- Identify and control psychosocial hazards: workload, rosters, isolation, trauma exposure, harassment.
- Train leaders to design work well and to recognise early signs of harm.

### 3.2 Promote
- Mental health awareness at induction and through yearly toolbox sessions (e.g. R U OK? Day, Mental Health Month in October).
- A culture where it is safe to speak up, with leaders openly supporting help-seeking.
- Access to flexible work arrangements where operations allow.

### 3.4 Respond
**a) When someone appears to be struggling**
1. Notice changes: withdrawal, irritability, errors, absenteeism, fatigue.
2. Approach privately and ask how they are (R U OK? approach: Ask, Listen, Encourage action, Check in).
3. Encourage them to use the EAP, see their GP, or speak with a Mental Health First Aider.
4. Consider whether work factors are contributing. If so, review the controls (OHS-010 §7).
5. Consider whether the person is fit for safety-critical tasks (e.g. crane operation, lid removal), handling this sensitively. Adjust duties temporarily if needed, without penalty.
6. Respect privacy. Share information only with consent, unless there is a serious and imminent risk to someone's life or safety.

**b) If there is immediate risk of suicide or harm:** stay with the person, call **000**, remove access to means if safe, and contact the Facility Manager.

**c) Critical incident response** (fire, explosion, serious injury, death or near miss)
| Timeframe | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| Immediately | Safety first. Make sure people are physically safe and their basic needs are met. Psychological first aid: calm, connect, inform. Do not force people to tell what happened. |
| Within 24–72 h | Contact the EAP for on-site or phone critical incident support. Offer it to everyone involved, including witnesses and contractors. Give practical support (transport home, time off). |
| 1 week | Facility Manager checks in individually. |
| 1 month | Follow-up check-in. Refer to the EAP or a GP if symptoms persist. |
| Ongoing | Review psychosocial controls for traumatic events (OHS-010). Be sensitive about the timing of investigation interviews ([OHS-004](ohs-004-incident-reporting-and-investigation.md) §6.2). |

### 3.5 Recover
Support for return to work after a psychological injury is in [OHS-005](ohs-005-injury-management-and-return-to-work.md) §5.4.

## 4. Responsibilities
| Role | Responsibility |
|------|----------------|
| Operations Director | Funds the program and leads by example. |
| Facility Manager | Manages the EAP contract, promotes the program and coordinates critical incident response. |
| Facility Manager | Checks in regularly, responds supportively, and refers to support. |
| Workers | Look out for each other and seek help early. |

## 5. Measuring Effectiveness
De-identified EAP usage and themes; psychosocial survey results; psychological injury claims; unplanned leave trends; participation in training. These are reviewed yearly at management review.
