## 1. Policy Statement
Reclaim Resources does not tolerate workplace bullying, sexual harassment, discrimination, victimisation or gendered violence. Everyone has the right to be treated with dignity and respect. We will take proactive steps to prevent this behaviour, and will respond promptly, fairly and confidentially to reports of it.

## 2. Scope
Covers all employees, managers, labour-hire workers, contractors, visitors, suppliers and transport drivers, at the facility and at work-related events, on work travel, and online (email, messaging, social media) where the behaviour is connected to work.

## 3. Definitions
| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Workplace bullying** | Repeated unreasonable behaviour directed toward an employee or group of employees that creates a risk to health and safety (WorkSafe Victoria). Examples: verbal abuse, belittling, excluding or isolating someone, giving pointless or impossible tasks, deliberately withholding information needed for work (e.g. shift handover information), spreading rumours, hazing new starters, and unjustified threats of dismissal. |
| **Not bullying** | **Reasonable management action carried out in a reasonable way**, for example fair performance management, allocating work, rostering, and disciplinary processes. A single incident of unreasonable behaviour is not bullying, but it can still be a hazard and should be reported. |
| **Sexual harassment** | Unwelcome conduct of a sexual nature where a reasonable person would anticipate the possibility that the person harassed would be offended, humiliated or intimidated (*Equal Opportunity Act 2010* (Vic) s.92; *Sex Discrimination Act 1984* (Cth)). It can be a single incident. Examples: sexual comments or jokes, unwanted touching, displaying sexual images, intrusive questions about someone's private life, repeated requests for dates. |
| **Gendered violence** | Any behaviour directed at, or affecting, a person because of their sex, sexual orientation, gender or gender identity, or because they do not follow socially prescribed gender roles. |
| **Discrimination** | Unfavourable treatment because of a protected attribute under equal opportunity law. |
| **Victimisation** | Subjecting or threatening someone with a detriment because they made a complaint, helped someone else to complain, or acted as a witness. **Victimisation is prohibited.** |

## 4. Legal Context
- **OHS Act 2004** s.21: duty to provide a working environment without risks to health, including psychological health.
- **OHS (Psychological Health) Regulations 2025**: bullying, sexual harassment and gendered violence are psychosocial hazards that must be identified and controlled (r.14–16).
- **Equal Opportunity Act 2010 (Vic)** and **Sex Discrimination Act 1984 (Cth)**: a **positive duty** to take reasonable and proportionate measures to eliminate sexual harassment, discrimination and victimisation, as far as possible.
- **Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth)**: workers may apply to the Fair Work Commission for an order to stop bullying or sexual harassment.

WorkSafe Victoria and the Victorian Equal Opportunity and Human Rights Commission (VEOHRC) regulate work-related sexual harassment together.

## 5. Prevention
Bullying and sexual harassment are managed as psychosocial hazards through [OHS-010](ohs-010-psychosocial-risk-management.md) and a site-wide [F-009 prevention plan](../../forms/f09-psychosocial-prevention-plan.md). Key controls:
- **Work design and systems:** clear role descriptions and authorities; fair and transparent rostering; structured shift handover (F-007) so information cannot be withheld; workloads that reduce conflict.
- **Environment:** separate, lockable amenities and change rooms; good lighting; no offensive material on site; codes of conduct for contractors and drivers.
- **Leadership:** managers model respectful behaviour and step in early on low-level incivility.
- **Supporting information and training:** induction; respectful workplace training every 2 years; bystander training; leader training on handling reports.

## 7. Response Procedure
| Step | Action | Timeframe |
|------|--------|-----------|
| 1. Receive | Listen and take the report seriously. Record the facts (F-004, confidential section). Explain the options and support available (EAP, [OHS-008](ohs-008-mental-health-and-support-program.md)). | On receipt |
| 2. Immediate safety | If there is a risk to safety, take interim measures: separate shifts or work areas, or paid leave pending investigation. These measures must **not disadvantage the person who reported**. Call police if a crime is involved. | Same day |
| 3. Assess | The HR Manager decides how to respond. If the HR Manager is involved, or acted as Contact Officer for this matter, the Operations Director decides instead. They take the reporter's wishes into account where possible. | Within 2 working days |
| 4a. Informal resolution | Suitable for low-level matters, with the agreement of the person reporting. Options: a facilitated discussion, a direct conversation, or mediation. Never required for sexual harassment or serious matters. | Within 10 working days |
| 4b. Formal investigation | For serious, repeated or sexual harassment matters, or when informal resolution fails. Carried out by an impartial investigator (external for serious matters or where senior staff are involved), following procedural fairness: the respondent is told the allegations and can respond, with a support person. Uses a trauma-informed approach. Findings are made on the balance of probabilities. | Target: within 30 days |
| 5. Outcome | Tell both parties the outcome (with appropriate privacy). Actions may include training, a warning, changes to work arrangements, or disciplinary action up to dismissal. If a contractor is involved, they may be removed from site. | On completion |
| 6. Review controls | Review the psychosocial controls as required by OHS-010 §7 (**a report of a psychosocial hazard is a mandatory trigger under r.16**). Address any systemic causes. | Within 1 month |
| 7. Follow up | Check on the wellbeing of the people involved, and monitor for victimisation. | 1 and 3 months |

**Confidentiality:** information is shared only with those who need to know to resolve the matter, or as required by law. **False complaints** made maliciously are a disciplinary matter, but a complaint that is not substantiated is not the same as a false complaint.
