## 1. Purpose
To eliminate, or reduce so far as is reasonably practicable, the risk of musculoskeletal disorders (MSDs) from hazardous manual handling. This procedure meets the OHS Regulations 2017 Part 3.1 (r.26–28) and follows the WorkSafe *Compliance code: Hazardous manual handling*.

## 2. Definition
**Hazardous manual handling** (r.5) is work that uses force exerted by a person to lift, lower, push, pull, carry, move, hold or restrain a thing (or a person or animal), **and** that involves:
- repetitive or sustained force;
- high or sudden force;
- repetitive movement;
- sustained or awkward posture; or
- exposure to vibration;

or handling unstable or unbalanced loads, or loads that are difficult to grasp or hold.

## 4. Risk Assessment
For each task, assess the risk with the Appendix A checklist. Consider (r.27(5)): **postures, movements, forces, duration and frequency, and environmental conditions** (heat near the kiln and pot, cold, vibration). Also consider task layout, load characteristics, work organisation (pace, breaks, staffing), and individual factors (the worker's experience, any injury history, PPE such as heat gloves that reduce grip).

Consult workers who do the task and the HSR (OHS Act s.35).

## 5. Control (r.27)
1. **Eliminate** the risk, so far as reasonably practicable. Examples: automate the task, or have bales and bags delivered in crane-liftable form.
2. Otherwise, **reduce** the risk by:
   - **altering the workplace layout** (working heights, platforms, reach distances);
   - **altering the workplace environment** (heat shielding, cooling, lighting, vibration);
   - **altering the systems of work** (task rotation, team lifting only as a last resort, pace, breaks);
   - **changing the things handled** (smaller containers, handles, lighter hoses);
   - **using mechanical aids** (crane, forklift, pallet jack, drum trolley, tool balancer, torque-reaction arm, valve gearbox);
   - a combination of these.
3. Then reduce any remaining risk with **information, instruction or training** (task-specific technique, using aids). Training may only be relied on **solely or mainly** when none of the measures in point 2 are reasonably practicable (r.27(4)).
4. Make sure mechanical aids are maintained, available and used.

## 6. Review (r.28)
Review the controls, and revise them if needed:
- before any change to a thing, process or system of work involving hazardous manual handling, including a change of location;
- when new information becomes available;
- when an MSD is reported;
- after a notifiable incident involving manual handling;
- when the controls do not adequately control the risk;
- when an HSR requests a review.

## 7. Reporting
Report discomfort, pain or injury **early**, on F-004 or to the Facility Manager. Early reporting lets the task be fixed before a serious injury happens. Report early symptoms to the RTW Coordinator so early intervention can begin ([OHS-005](ohs-005-injury-management-and-return-to-work.md)).

## 8. Records
Manual handling risk assessments (Appendix A), training records, and records of reviews.
