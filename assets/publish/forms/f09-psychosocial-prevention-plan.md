> [!NOTE]
> A prevention plan is a written record of the risk management process for psychosocial hazards. WorkSafe recommends prevention plans but does not make them mandatory; the duty to identify, control and review is mandatory (OHS (Psychological Health) Regulations 2025 r.14–16).
>
> - Use **one site-wide plan** for hazards that are common across the site with similar controls.
> - Use **separate area or role plans** where hazards or exposures differ, e.g. control room night shift, or operators exposed to traumatic events.
> - **Do not include personal details or anything that could identify individual employees.**

## Step 3: Control Risks
*Compliance code reference: eliminating and reducing risk (Part 3, Step 3); example controls (Appendix C).*

> **Regulation 15:** First eliminate the risk, so far as reasonably practicable. Otherwise reduce it by **altering** the management of work, plant, systems of work, work design or workplace environment. **Information, instruction or training must not be the predominant control.** It may be used on its own only where no alteration is reasonably practicable; record why in the "Control type" column.

## Step 4: Review and Revise
*Compliance code reference: reviewing and revising risk controls (Part 3, Step 4).*

**Mandatory review triggers (r.16):** before a change likely to affect psychosocial risk; when there is new information about a hazard; when a psychological injury or psychosocial hazard is reported; after a notifiable incident involving a psychosocial hazard; when controls are not working; when an HSR requests a review.
